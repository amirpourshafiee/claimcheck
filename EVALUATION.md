# Evaluation report

Test split, run once on 2026-11-23 after the judge prompt (v1), the model, the baseline threshold
and every label were frozen. Nothing was tuned on it. Numbers below come straight from
`results/*_test.json`; the paired comparison is reproducible with `python -m claimcheck.compare`.

## Question

Given an AI answer, its cited sources and nothing else, can a cheap model flag the claims the
cited source does not support, better than a trivial word-overlap baseline?

## Setup

- **Data.** 76 test claims from 8 answers: 48 organic (taken from the chatbot's answers as
  written) and 28 planted (failures written by the annotator). See [DATASHEET.md](DATASHEET.md).
- **Positive class.** "Flagged", meaning any label except `SUPPORTED`. Precision, recall and F1
  are for that class.
- **Judges.** Claude Haiku 5.5 with the v1 prompt (it sees only the cited excerpt, must quote it,
  and fabricated quotes are discarded), and a lexical baseline (word overlap at 0.7 plus number
  matching, never predicts `CONTRADICTED`, threshold never tuned).
- **Two views.** `end_to_end` includes planted fake citations, which a plain lookup catches
  without any model (7 of 7 caught, 0 false alarms). `judge_only` removes them. Organic claims
  contain no fake citations, so the organic rows are identical in both views.
- **Intervals.** 95% percentile bootstrap, resampling whole answers (8 answers).

## Results

Headline row: the judge on **organic** claims, because planted claims are easy (see below).

| Judge | Slice | n | Gold flagged | Precision | Recall | F1 (95% CI) | kappa (4-class) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude Haiku 5.5 | organic | 48 | 9 | 0.89 | 0.89 | **0.89** [0.67, 1.00] | 0.74 |
| Word-overlap baseline | organic | 48 | 9 | 0.70 | 0.78 | 0.74 [0.50, 0.94] | 0.62 |
| Claude Haiku 5.5 | judge_only, all | 69 | 30 | 0.97 | 0.97 | 0.97 [0.92, 1.00] | 0.90 |
| Word-overlap baseline | judge_only, all | 69 | 30 | 0.90 | 0.90 | 0.90 [0.81, 0.97] | 0.66 |
| Claude Haiku 5.5 | planted, judge_only | 21 | 21 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 (no errors, interval uninformative) | 1.00 |
| Word-overlap baseline | planted, judge_only | 21 | 21 | 1.00 | 0.95 | 0.98 [0.92, 1.00] | 0.09 |

End to end (with the 7 fake citations): judge F1 0.97 [0.94, 1.00], baseline 0.92 [0.85, 0.98].

**Paired difference** (judge minus baseline, same resamples for both):

| Slice | F1 difference | 95% CI | Share of resamples with difference <= 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| organic | +0.15 | [0.00, +0.31] | 9.3% |
| judge_only, all | +0.07 | [+0.015, +0.146] | 2.3% |

### What these numbers support

- The LLM judge is better than word overlap on this data. On organic claims the gain is +0.15
  with an interval that just touches zero, so it is **suggestive, not established**. The all-claims
  gain of +0.07 excludes zero, but planted rows drive it and both judges nearly saturate there.
- Organic recall rests on **9 flagged claims**. One claim moves F1 by about 0.1, which is why the
  organic interval is wide.
- Planted claims are a weak test. A word-overlap baseline gets 0.98 F1 on them. Do not quote a
  planted or combined score as evidence the judge is good.
- The baseline's 4-class kappa on planted rows (0.09) is low only because it never says
  `CONTRADICTED`. The flagged-class metric does not penalize that, which is why it is primary.

## Error analysis (all four judge errors, all organic)

| Claim | Gold | Judge | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| q02-c04 "lithium-ion cell moves ions between a graphite anode and a metal oxide cathode" | CONTRADICTED | SUPPORTED | The excerpt says the negative electrode is usually graphite, the positive uses oxides, and Li+ intercalates reversibly. The annotator's own note says it "can be derived but it is not explicitly mentioned", which fits NOT_MENTIONED or SUPPORTED, not CONTRADICTED. **Flagged-class miss.** Likely a label error. The baseline missed it too. |
| q11-c06 "SZ40/42 keystream was generated by 12 wheels" | SUPPORTED | NOT_MENTIONED | The excerpt gives twelve rotors for the definitive SZ40, ten for the first SZ40, and nothing for the SZ42. The judge's strictness is defensible. Two other raters labeled it SUPPORTED. **Flagged-class false alarm.** A genuine ambiguity. |
| q06-c03 "a guide RNA directs Cas9" | CONTRADICTED | NOT_MENTIONED | The excerpt says CRISPR sequences guide Cas9 and never says "guide RNA". Nothing contradicts it. Both labels count as flagged, so the F1 is unaffected. Likely a label error. |
| q12-c03 "about 56 hours into the flight, an oxygen tank exploded" | CONTRADICTED | NOT_MENTIONED | The excerpt says "two days into the mission". Whether "about 56 hours" contradicts that is a judgment call. Both flagged, so F1 is unaffected. |

Reading: only q02-c04 (miss) and q11-c06 (false alarm) affect the headline F1, and both are
disputable. I think the annotator over-used CONTRADICTED when the source was silent or only
approximately different (this also happened with q03-c03, corrected before the test run). That
inflates the `CONTRADICTED` row of the confusion matrix and lowers the 4-class kappa, but it does
not change the flagged-class result, which is why that metric leads.

**These labels were not changed after the test run.** Doing so would mean tuning the answer key to
the judge's output. If a future version corrects them, it must say so and report both numbers.

## What this does not show

- **Small and narrow.** 76 test claims, 8 answers, 9 flagged organic claims, short Wikipedia
  excerpts about science and engineering history. No claim about other domains or long documents.
- **One annotator, loosely checked.** Two language-model raters labeled 20 claims (not blind to
  the labels in one case, and one of them wrote the original answers) and agreed with the annotator
  on 19 and 18 of 20 (datasheet). The annotator never relabeled their
  own claims blind, so repeatability is unmeasured.
- **The labeling rules and the judge prompt are the same rules.** The judge is graded against an
  answer key written by someone applying the judge's own instructions.
- **Wikipedia is in the model's training data.** The judge could answer from memory. The quote
  check limits this but does not prove it never happens.
- **One chatbot wrote every answer** and the same chat split them into claims.
- **Extraction (stage 1) is not evaluated.** Claims were split by that chatbot and checked by hand.
  The extractor in the pipeline is tested only with scripted replies, not measured on real answers.

## Cost and reliability

- Test run: 69 paid calls, **$0.020** measured, 5,441 thinking tokens (the model reasons by
  default and those tokens are billed as output). No unparseable replies.
- All paid runs for the project cost about $0.03 in total. A hard call cap and a printed estimate
  guard every paid run, and the test split cannot run without `--final`.
- One real bug surfaced on the first dev run: the model sometimes answered with a valid JSON
  verdict, then unprompted text, then a second JSON object. The parser now accepts several objects
  only if their labels agree, and one bad reply no longer aborts a run (it is counted and scored
  as not flagged). It did not recur on the test split.
